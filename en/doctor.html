Meet Dr. Cho

Board-certified cardiologist with a PhD in arrhythmia research

Jae Hyung Cho, MD, PhD

Cardiologist

Dr. Jae Hyung Cho is a cardiologist providing comprehensive care to adults with a wide range of heart conditions, including hypertension, hyperlipidemia, angina, myocardial infarction, heart failure, and cardiac arrhythmias.

Dr. Cho completed his internal medicine residency at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign in 2013 and his cardiology fellowship at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center in 2021, where he also earned a PhD with a focus on cardiac arrhythmia research. He has served as a Clinical Assistant Professor at the Cleveland Clinic and is currently a member of the medical staff at Cedars-Sinai Medical Center.

Research & academic work

Publications

  • Author of the chapter “Gene and cell therapy for the treatment of cardiac arrhythmia” in the medical textbook Arytmier
  • Research articles in Nature, Circulation, and the European Heart Journal on heart disease, mechanisms of heart failure, and AI-based assessment of cardiac function
  • Review articles in JACC: Clinical Electrophysiology, Circulation: Arrhythmia and Electrophysiology, the Journal of Cardiovascular Electrophysiology, and the Korean Circulation Journal on arrhythmia mechanisms, pacemaker-related arrhythmias, and sudden death in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF)
  • Research articles in PLoS One, the American Journal of Medicine, BMC Cardiovascular Disorders, and JMCC Plus on arrhythmias in COVID-19 patients, echocardiographic prognostic factors in pulmonary embolism, and dietary management in heart failure
  • Numerous case reports and research articles in the Journal of Neuroscience, Archives of Ophthalmology, BMJ Case Reports, BMC Research Notes, the Heart Research Open Journal, and Carle Selected Papers

Presentations & invited talks

  • Invited talks at the Cedars-Sinai Smidt Heart Institute seminar and the Heart Rhythm Society (HRS) Scientific Sessions on ventricular arrhythmias in HFpEF and treatment approaches
  • Numerous presentations at American College of Cardiology (ACC) and American Heart Association (AHA) meetings on HFpEF animal models, cardiosphere-derived cell therapy, arrhythmias in hospitalized COVID-19 patients, cardiac amyloidosis, and outcomes after percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI)
  • Presentations at HRS Scientific Sessions on sudden death, heart rate variability, rhythm abnormalities, and the effects of cardiac-derived cell therapy in HFpEF models
  • Clinical research presented at the American College of Physicians (ACP), American Society of Nephrology (ASN), and Society of Hospital Medicine (SHM) on right-ventricular and diastolic dysfunction as prognostic factors in acute pulmonary embolism, hyperkalemia, and thrombosis after aortic dissection surgery
  • Presentations at Basic Cardiovascular Sciences (BCVS) on the effect of a high-salt diet on blood cholesterol and of dietary management on HFpEF progression
